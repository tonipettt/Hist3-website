Community and Everyday Life

Exploring routines, interaction, and lived experiences through historical analysis.

Together at the Summit

This moment shows the class coming together after the climb. It reflects how shared experiences can build connection, trust, and a stronger sense of belonging.

Figure 1. Students gathered at the summit during the immersion.

Sharing Stories and Traditions

The warmth of the I’waks community was felt not just through their traditions, but in the way they welcomed visitors into their homes and everyday lives. Simple moments of sharing meals, conversations, and daily activities showed how their community is built on hospitality, respect, and strong connections with one another.

Figure 2. A nighttime gathering while food is being prepared.

Life Built on Hillsides

The houses and walkways show how daily life adjusts to the mountain setting. The community is shaped by the land, and the land also shapes how people move, meet, and live each day.

Figure 3. Homes and pathways built along the hillside.

A community in Motion

Students from the University of the Philippines Baguio engage in a traditional Mountain Province dance with Kayapa locals, an embodied encounter with living heritage. This immersion bridges classroom learning and the everyday realities of Indigenous communities, revealing how cultural continuity thrives through shared participation. .

Figure 4. Students joining an activity near the barangay hall.

Volleyball with a view

Seeing locals play volleyball reminds me that history is not only written in textbooks or monuments but lived through gestures of cooperation, resilience, and joy. In this court, I see how the people of Kayapa weave modern recreation into their lifeways, transforming sport into a symbol of adaptation and continuity.

Figure 6. A light moment during the visit.
