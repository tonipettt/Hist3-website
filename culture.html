Culture Identity Historical Memory

Understanding how traditions, stories, and shared experiences preserve community identity.

Living Knowledge

During the gathering, the Barangay head explained the process of bile reading while people listened using flashlights and smartphones around them. The moment showed that Indigenous knowledge in Kayapa is not frozen in the past, but continues to be practiced, shared, and adapted in everyday life.

Figure 1. A symbol of local identity.

A community at work

This scene shows culture through shared action. Work, movement, and cooperation become part of how a community expresses who they are.

Figure 2. A moment of shared work in the community.

Ritual Site Unmoved

Upon seeing this while on the hike, it really warmed and healed my heart for that the site where rituals were once held is still here and preserve. This tells me that the people had a rich culture on rituals, and that even after all this time, it remains there as a reminder of what once was. This makes me want to question and learn on what the rituals were that was done in this circle of rocks and the stories that was lost within this site. This is not to point out that they had “exotic rituals”, rather show that they had a rich history, and would it be possible to uncover their cultural rituals once more?

Figure 3. A landscape connected to local meaning.

Cultures lives on children too

Culture continues when children learn, join, and take part in community life. Their presence shows how traditions are passed on in simple everyday moments.

Figure 4. Children taking part in the life of the community.

Shared Grounds

The image of two cows standing side by side reflects the idea that difference is often something imposed by people rather than nature itself. During the immersion, the communities of Kayapa showed that Indigenous identity is not something lesser or separate, but part of a shared humanity shaped by connection to the land, animals, and one another
