Labor and Economic Life

Exploring work, survival, and economic systems through historical analysis.

Cooking over open flame

Cooking over an open fire shows how work begins with basic needs. It reminds us that labor is not always formal; sometimes it is the everyday effort of preparing food and caring for others.

Figure 1. Food being prepared over an open flame.

No picking of wild plants

This sign shows how the community protects the resources around them. It reminds visitors that nature is part of daily life, and taking from it also comes with responsibility.

Figure 2. A reminder to protect wild plants in the area.

Working the river

The river is more than part of the scenery. For many communities, water is connected to travel, food, washing, and other daily tasks that keep life moving.

Figure 3. Work and daily life connected to the river.

Boats at rest, Lives in Motion

The resting boats show a quiet side of labor. Even when no one is moving, the tools of work are still present and ready for the next trip, task, or livelihood.

Figure 4. Boats resting near the water after use.
Figure 5. Women of Kayapa preparing tapuey together.

Women of Tapuey Making

In Kayapa, women lead the making of tapuey, sharing laughs, stories, and steady hands as they work. Their role shows how everyday labor is shaped by experience and trust, not fixed ideas about who should do what.
