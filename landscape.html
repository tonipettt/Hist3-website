Landscapes and Historical Memory

Exploring memory, place, and historical meaning through landscapes.

Standing on MT.Tugew

Standing on Mt. Tugew gives a wider view of the land and the community around it. The height of the place helps show how landscape can hold memory, experience, and belonging.

Figure 1. Students standing on Mt. Tugew during the visit.

Morning mist over the Valley

The morning mist gives the valley a quiet feeling. It shows how the landscape changes with time of day, weather, and the way people remember the place.

Figure 2. Morning mist covering the valley landscape.

A landscape shaped by time and weather

The land carries marks of rain, wind, movement, and use. These changes show that landscapes are not still; they continue to change with nature and community life.

Figure 3. A landscape marked by time and weather.

The forest as a living Heritage

The forest is part of the community's living heritage. It gives shade, resources, stories, and a sense of place that connects people to their surroundings.

Figure 4. A forested area that forms part of local heritage.

The map on the wall

The map helps make the place easier to understand. It turns paths, landmarks, and boundaries into a guide for remembering and moving through the community.

Figure 5. A wall map showing the area and its landmarks.
