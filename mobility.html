Mobility and Movement

Understanding transportation, migration, and movement in historical spaces.

Arriving in Kayapa

The jeepney that brought the class to Kayapa also carried the supplies and belongings needed for the trip. Watching everyone unload together showed how transportation made the immersion possible, connecting students to the community and to the experiences that awaited them

Figure 1. The jeepney used during the immersion trip.

Routes of Adaptation

During the immersion, the jeepneys and roads of Kayapa showed how transportation is shaped around the needs of the community and the terrain they travel through every day. Rather than replacing tradition, these forms of mobility revealed how the people of Kayapa continue to adapt to change while still remaining connected to their environment and way of life.

Figure 2. A road leading into the mountain community.

Riding Through the Trail

During the hike to Mount Tugew, horseback riding revealed how both people and animals have learned to move through the difficult terrain of Kayapa with familiarity and trust. What may seem like a simple horseback adventure to visitors is actually part of a deeper relationship built through everyday interaction with the land and its trails.

Figure 3. A horse used as transport in the area.

Old Paths, New Trails

The presence of new trails in Kayapa does not erase the culture and history already rooted in the land. As places continue to change and develop, the people of Kayapa continue to preserve and highlight their identity, showing that movement forward does not mean forgetting where they began.

Figure 4. A signpost marking directions along the route.

The path made for people

This path shows how mobility also happens on foot. Simple walkways help people move through the community and connect homes, work, and gathering spaces.

Figure 5. A footpath used for everyday movement.
