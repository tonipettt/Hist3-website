Mobility and Movement

Understanding transportation, migration, and movement in historical spaces.

The jeepney that brought us here

The jeepney made the trip possible and connected the class to the community. It shows how transportation shapes access, movement, and the start of the immersion experience.

Figure 1. The jeepney used during the immersion trip.

The road into the mountains

The mountain road shows that movement is not always easy. Curved paths and long travel times shape how people reach schools, work, markets, and neighboring places.

Figure 2. A road leading into the mountain community.

Horse as transport

The horse shows another way people move through difficult terrain. In places where roads are limited, animals can still help carry people, goods, and daily needs.

Figure 3. A horse used as transport in the area.

Signpost along the way

Signposts guide visitors and locals through unfamiliar routes. They make movement easier and show how places are connected by names, directions, and shared paths.

Figure 4. A signpost marking directions along the route.

The path made for people

This path shows how mobility also happens on foot. Simple walkways help people move through the community and connect homes, work, and gathering spaces.

Figure 5. A footpath used for everyday movement.
